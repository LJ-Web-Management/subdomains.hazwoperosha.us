What is HAZWOPER?

Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response: the OSHA standard at 29 CFR 1910.120 (and 1926.65 for construction) covering cleanup sites, treatment, storage and disposal facilities, and emergency response to hazardous substance releases.

Do I need the 40-hour or the 24-hour?

The 40-hour is for general site workers who may be exposed at or above exposure limits or need respirators. The 24-hour is for workers on site only occasionally for limited tasks, or in monitored areas below exposure limits without respirators.

Is online HAZWOPER training valid?

Online courses can cover the classroom hours. The initial courses also require supervised field experience on site (3 days for the 40-hour, 1 day for the 24-hour), which your employer provides.

How long does the 40-hour take?

40 hours of instruction. Online and self-paced, many people finish in one to two weeks working a few hours a day.

Do supervisors need extra training?

Yes. Supervisors need the same initial training as their workers plus at least 8 hours of specialized supervisor training.

What training do emergency responders need?

It depends on the role: awareness, operations, technician, specialist or incident commander, each with its own requirements under 1910.120(q)(6).

What about workers at a TSD facility?

Workers at permitted treatment, storage and disposal facilities need 24 hours of initial training and 8 hours of refresher training each year under paragraph (p).

How often do I need a refresher?

Eight hours of refresher training every year (12 months).

What if my refresher lapsed?

You're out of compliance until you complete one. For a short lapse, take the refresher right away. After a long lapse, your employer may require the initial course again.

Can I take the refresher early?

Yes. Your next refresher is then due 12 months from the new date.

Is there a grace period?

The federal standard doesn't provide one. Plan renewals ahead.

Do I get a certificate?

Yes. Each course provides a certificate of completion. Keep copies of every certificate, including refreshers.

Does my certificate expire?

The initial certificate stays valid as long as you keep up the annual refreshers.

Will my certificate work in another state?

The hours are the same everywhere. A few state plans write their own HAZWOPER rule, such as California, Washington and Michigan, and employers there may want state-specific refresher training.

Can my employer accept training from another company?

Yes. Employers can accept documented equivalent training and experience under 1910.120(e)(9).

Is there a course in Spanish?

Yes. The OSHA 40-Hour HAZWOPER is available in Spanish.

Can I take the course on my phone?

Yes. The courses work on phones, tablets and computers, and progress saves as you go.

What are the PPE levels?

Level A (fully encapsulating suit and SCBA), Level B (SCBA with splash protection), Level C (air-purifying respirator with chemical-resistant clothing) and Level D (work uniform).

What are the site zones?

Exclusion zone (hot), contamination reduction zone (warm, where decon happens) and support zone (cold).

What is a site safety and health plan?

The written, site-specific plan required by 1910.120(b)(4), covering hazards, training, PPE, medical surveillance, monitoring, site control, decon, emergencies, confined spaces and spill containment.

Do I need medical surveillance?

Covered workers include those exposed at or above limits 30 or more days a year, respirator wearers for 30 or more days a year, HAZMAT team members, and workers injured or ill from possible overexposure.

Does HAZWOPER apply to every spill?

No. Incidental releases that people in the area can safely control aren't emergency responses under HAZWOPER.
