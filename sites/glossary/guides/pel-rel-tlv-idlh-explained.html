PEL, REL, TLV, STEL and IDLH: exposure limits explained

Five acronyms for exposure limits, who sets each one and which one your site plan uses.

LimitSet byMeansLegally binding?
PELOSHAPermissible exposure limit, usually an 8-hour TWAYes
RELNIOSHRecommended exposure limit, often lower than the PELNo, it's a recommendation
TLVACGIHThreshold limit value, updated regularlyNo, unless adopted by a rule
STELOSHA, NIOSH or ACGIH15-minute short-term limitYes when it's an OSHA STEL
CeilingOSHA, NIOSH or ACGIHNever to be exceeded, even brieflyYes when it's an OSHA ceiling
IDLHNIOSHImmediately dangerous to life or healthUsed for respirator selection

Why there are so many

Many OSHA PELs date from the early 1970s and haven't been updated. NIOSH and ACGIH publish newer, often lower, values. California updates its own PELs more often than federal OSHA.

What your site plan uses

Many site safety plans set action levels using the lowest of the PEL, REL and TLV, to stay protective. Your plan's action levels are the numbers to follow.

IDLH and respirators

IDLH conditions require the most protective respirators: SCBA or supplied air with an escape bottle. Air-purifying respirators (Level C) aren't allowed in IDLH atmospheres.
