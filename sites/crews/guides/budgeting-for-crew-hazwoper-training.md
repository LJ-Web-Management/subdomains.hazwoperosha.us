# Budgeting for crew HAZWOPER training

The course fee is the smallest line. A realistic budget for training a crew.

When contractors price HAZWOPER training, they usually price the course. The bigger costs are elsewhere.

## The full cost of a new 40-hour worker

| Item | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Course seat | $249.99 for [OSHA 40 Hour HAZWOPER Training](https://crews.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training) |
| Paid training time | 40 hours of wages, often the largest single cost |
| Field experience | 3 days under a supervisor, often on a real job |
| Medical exam | Before assignment, if covered by medical surveillance |
| Respirator fit test | Before respirator use and every 12 months |
| PPE | Boots, gloves, suits and respirator |

## The yearly cost of a certified worker

- [OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER Refresher Training](https://crews.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-8-hour-hazwoper-refresher-training) at $59.99 per seat
- 8 hours of paid time
- Annual fit test and, for most, an annual medical exam

## Ways to save

1. **Online courses.** No travel and no trainer day rate, and workers can train on slow days or between jobs.
2. **One renewal month.** Line up refreshers, fit tests and medicals for the whole crew in the same month.
3. **Train the right level.** Not everyone needs 40 hours. Workers who qualify can take [OSHA 24 Hour HAZWOPER Training](https://crews.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-24-hour-hazwoper-training).
4. **Build field days into real work.** The first project a new hire works on can be their supervised field experience.
5. **An in-house trainer** pays off for larger crews. See [HAZWOPER Train-the-Trainer Certification](https://crews.hazwoperosha.us/courses/hazwoper-train-the-trainer-certification).

## Plan for turnover

Budget for new hires through the year, not just the start-of-season crew.
