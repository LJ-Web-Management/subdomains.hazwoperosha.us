# Cartridge change schedules

Why 'change it when you smell it' isn't allowed.

For gases and vapors, OSHA requires air-purifying respirators to have either an **end-of-service-life indicator** or a **change schedule** based on objective data.

## Building a schedule

Use the manufacturer's service-life software or data, with:

- The contaminants and their concentrations (from air monitoring, see [OSHA Air Monitoring and Gas Detection Training](https://respirator-muc.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-air-monitoring-and-gas-detection-training))
- Temperature and humidity
- Work rate and breathing rate

## Then

- Write the schedule into the respiratory protection program
- Build in a safety margin
- Change cartridges at least as often as the schedule says, or at the end of every shift if that's sooner for your conditions

## Don't rely on odor

Many chemicals have poor warning properties, or are only detectable by smell above their exposure limit.
