Tracking training renewal cycles across your workforce

Every rule has its own clock. A reference table for the cycles EHS teams track most.

The hardest part of training compliance isn't the training. It's the calendar. Each rule renews on a different cycle.

Common renewal cycles

RequirementInitialRenewal
HAZWOPER site workers (1910.120(e))40 or 24 hours before work8 hours every 12 months
HAZWOPER supervisors+8 hours before supervisingWithin the annual refresher
HAZWOPER responders (q)By level before respondingAnnually, or demonstrated competency
TSDF workers (p)24 hours8 hours every 12 months
RCRA LQG personnelWithin 6 months of assignmentAnnual review
DOT hazmat employeesWithin 90 daysAt least every 3 years
Respirator fit test (1910.134)Before useEvery 12 months
PSM operator refresher (1910.119)Before operatingAt least every 3 years
Medical surveillance (1910.120(f))Before assignmentUsually every 12 months

How to track it

  1. Build a training matrix: people down the side, requirements across the top, due dates in the cells.
  2. Flag anything due within 60 days.
  3. Align renewals where you can. For example, put the whole crew's HAZWOPER refresher and respirator fit test in the same month.
  4. Keep certificates attached to the matrix, not in a separate drawer.

Multi-state programs

If you operate in state-plan states, check whether the state has its own HAZWOPER rule. 8 Hour HAZWOPER Refresher - State-Plan Versions Available covers federal and state-plan equivalents in one refresher.
