Common hazardous wastes in hospitals, department by department

Hazardous waste isn't just in the lab. A walk through where it's generated in a typical hospital.

When people think of hospital waste, they think of red bags and sharps. Those are regulated medical waste, mostly under state rules. Hazardous waste under RCRA is a separate stream, and it shows up all over the building.

Department by department

DepartmentTypical hazardous waste
PharmacyCertain chemotherapy drugs, warfarin, nicotine products other than OTC replacement therapies, expired hazardous drugs
Pathology and histologyFormalin, xylene, alcohols, stains
Clinical labSolvents, reagents, mercury-containing compounds
RadiologyOld fixer and developer solutions, lead aprons and shielding
Facilities and maintenancePaints, solvents, used oil, aerosol cans
BiomedBatteries, mercury devices, circuit boards
Environmental servicesSome cleaning chemicals, mercury from broken thermometers
EverywhereFluorescent lamps and batteries (universal waste)

Why it matters

Mixing hazardous waste into regulated medical waste or trash is one of the most common healthcare violations, and incinerators for medical waste often aren't permitted for hazardous waste.

Make it simple for staff

  • Put the right container in the right place, labeled clearly.
  • Use color-coding: many facilities use black for RCRA hazardous pharmaceuticals, yellow for trace chemo and red for biohazard. Follow your facility's own scheme.
  • Train the people who fill containers, not only the people who ship them.
