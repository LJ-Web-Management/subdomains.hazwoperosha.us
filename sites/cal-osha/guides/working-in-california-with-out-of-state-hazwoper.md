# Moving to a California job with an out-of-state HAZWOPER certificate

Your 40-hour likely still counts. Here's what California employers check, and when a Cal/OSHA course is the smart move.

Crews follow the work, and plenty of California HAZWOPER jobs go to people trained in Texas, Nevada or Arizona. Do you need to start over? Usually not.

## What Cal/OSHA looks at

Cal/OSHA doesn't keep a list of approved providers or reject certificates from other states. 8 CCR 5192 asks whether the worker's training covered the required topics and hours, and whether the employer can show it. Like the federal rule, it lets an employer accept equivalent prior training if they document it.

So a valid federal 40-hour certificate plus a current refresher is generally a sound starting point.

## Where gaps show up

- **Exposure limits.** Your training taught federal PELs. California's are often lower. Your new site's safety plan will use the California numbers.
- **Heat illness.** California's heat rule is specific about water, shade, rest and acclimatization. Expect a site-specific briefing.
- **Agencies.** Spills and waste go to DTSC, the CUPA, the Regional Water Board and the local fire department, not just "the EPA."

## The simple fix

When your next annual refresher comes up, take [Cal/OSHA 8-Hour HAZWOPER Refresher Training](https://cal-osha.hazwoperosha.us/courses/cal-osha-8-hour-hazwoper-refresher-training). It covers the 8-hour annual requirement and fills in the California differences. Your new certificate then references the rule your inspector will cite.

## If your certificate has lapsed

If your refresher is more than 12 months old, you're out of compliance until you finish one. If it's been years, your new employer may ask you to retake the initial course. In that case take [Cal/OSHA 40-Hour HAZWOPER Training (2026)](https://cal-osha.hazwoperosha.us/courses/cal-osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training), or [Cal/OSHA 24-Hour HAZWOPER Training](https://cal-osha.hazwoperosha.us/courses/cal-osha-24-hour-hazwoper-training) if your role is limited-exposure.

## Bring your records

Bring your initial certificate, every refresher, any supervisor certificate, your respirator fit test and your medical clearance. Missing paperwork is the most common reason a qualified worker gets sent back to class.
