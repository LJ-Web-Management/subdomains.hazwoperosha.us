# When in doubt, why most employers choose the 40-hour

The 24-hour is narrower than people think. Why the 40-hour is usually the safer choice.

The 24-hour is allowed only for workers whose exposure really is limited. In practice, many employers default to the 40-hour. Here's why.

## Conditions change

A site that's characterized as low-exposure today can turn up a drum or a hot spot tomorrow. Workers with only 24 hours can't step into those tasks.

## Respirators

Anyone who needs a respirator is outside the 24-hour categories.

## Portability

Many employers and site owners require the 40-hour for all site personnel. A 40-hour certificate opens more doors for a worker.

## The cost difference is small

The difference between [OSHA 24 Hour HAZWOPER Training](https://course-finder.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-24-hour-hazwoper-training) and [OSHA 40 Hour HAZWOPER Training](https://course-finder.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training) is 16 hours and two field days. Compared with being sent off a job because training doesn't fit the task, it's a small investment.

## When the 24-hour makes sense

Surveyors, inspectors, delivery drivers and workers who stay in monitored, characterized areas. If that's clearly your role, the 24-hour is the right fit.
