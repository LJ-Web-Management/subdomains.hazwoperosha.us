Munitions on cleanup sites: recognize, retreat, report

Former ranges and bases can hide unexploded ordnance. HAZWOPER crews aren't trained to handle it, and shouldn't.

Former military ranges and training areas can contain munitions and explosives of concern (MEC), including unexploded ordnance (UXO). HAZWOPER training doesn't qualify anyone to handle them.

The 3Rs

DoD's explosives safety message for everyone:

  1. Recognize when you may have found a munition. It may be rusted, buried, partly exposed or look like scrap.
  2. Retreat. Don't touch, move or disturb it. Carefully leave the area the way you came.
  3. Report. Tell your supervisor and the site's UXO personnel, or call 911 off site.

How sites manage it

  • MEC surveys before intrusive work
  • UXO technicians escort crews or clear work areas on sites where munitions may be present
  • Construction support: qualified UXO personnel monitor digging
  • Briefings for every worker before entry

For supervisors

Know the site's MEC plan, who the UXO lead is and the stop-work procedure. Make sure every crew member has had the munitions awareness briefing. See OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER Supervisor Training.
